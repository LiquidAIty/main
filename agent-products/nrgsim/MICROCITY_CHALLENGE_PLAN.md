# Draft worldwide microcity modeling challenge

Create a bold, openly improvable architectural and EnergyPlus modeling playing field: a default microgrid-city scene that people can explore and modify, alongside the option to create their own building. The first model is an entry, not a predetermined winner. Participants should be able to improve design, materials, systems, controls or modeling methods and reproduce the comparison.

## Current owner direction

- Design focus: a deliberately provocative district-scale microgrid city targeting the world's tallest, widest, deepest and most useful interconnected building/city. These are targets, not achieved records. Define architectural/occupied height, continuous horizontal extent, footprint/GFA, occupied depth below local grade and useful service outputs separately; mountain overburden, foundation depth and occupied basement depth are not interchangeable. Approximately 1000 m remains a height research target, not demonstrated structural feasibility.
- Primary site direction: the Skid Row area of Los Angeles, adjoining the Fashion District; these are not identical map polygons. The owner explicitly chooses hypothetical district-wide acquisition/clearance/redevelopment and a development-site assumption for the design experiment. This is not an actual acquisition, current vacant-site claim, approval or instruction to act on residents/properties. Keep the modeled clean-site scenario distinct from any real-world acquisition, replacement/relocation, demolition and phasing assessment.
- The owner now allows many buildings, public streets and underground vehicle/service streets where useful. Do not force every function into one monolith. Three terraced buildings converging toward a shared skyplace remain a leading landmark candidate within the district; wall/pyramid alternatives remain. The earlier sphere/twelve-sided crown is no longer a requirement.
- Street-level life is essential: FashionDistrict-scale retail, shops, galleries, grocery, food, SROs, affordable apartments, market-rate apartments, larger homes/mansions, offices and cultural/event uses. Housing price bands are not automatically distinct thermal occupancy types.
- South-facing solar collection surfaces, exterior shade and controlled occupied glazing. Maximize useful collection, not uncontrolled heat gain.
- PV over planted roofs/terraces must include plant evapotranspiration, irrigation, drainage and maintenance in the study.
- Include a data center, electrically lit vertical farm and separately controlled daylit greenhouse. Their energy, water and humidity are not interchangeable.
- Include a major event-center ambition beyond Sphere: broader uses and a better experience/operational proposition, not an unsupported acoustics or attendance claim.
- Include virtual/video windows offering high-floor views in interior spaces. They are AV loads and amenities, not substitutes for actual daylight, ventilation or required openings.
- Include a new-air-taxi skyport and charging/support loads. Reserve two distinct aircraft-envelope alternatives: VTOL landing/parking areas and an optional linear short-takeoff deck/runway. A vertiport pad is not automatically a STOL runway. The skyplace concept should separate aviation access, charging/service and downwash/approach space from public gardens; exact dimensions and operations follow a named reference aircraft and specialist review.
- Include an underground rail station if reasonable for the site, with a public concourse connecting the microcity and event district. Distinguish real existing transit access from a hypothetical extension. Foundations, rail vibration, normal ventilation, emergency smoke control and evacuation are specialist interfaces; station energy and rail propulsion have explicitly separate study boundaries.
- LosAngeles is now the home-base study location because of the owner's professional network. LasVegas remains a useful alternate-climate/stress track. The relocate feature carries the same design among world locations with new site/weather/orientation cases and results; changing city does not preserve a claim that old SQL represents the new climate.
- Reuse the owner's old occupancy library. Do not invent that every occupancy has already been recovered or that all code/hazard classes can coexist in one tower.
- Keep the shared inspector's baseline-versus-one-performance view. More reference/entrant files may exist without displaying three competing panels.

## What is being built first

### Default scene and create your own

The intended front door has two routes: participate in one shared challenge city, or create a separate personal building/project. A user may remove the example from their own workspace and start blank; that must not delete the shared city or another user's work. This describes future product behavior and does not authorize deleting today's accepted NRGsim models. Existing recovered assets and accepted simulations remain separate until a runnable city seed exists.

SimCity-like means spatially understandable editing and visible, reproducible consequences. Rendering may preview geometry and sunlight; energy/comfort/heat-map results require an identified run. A pending run is not replaced with invented live numbers. The main app's saved-Card/inspector ownership remains unchanged.

Owner clarification: this is **not a SimCity clone**. The intended invitation is essentially: “Here is my best shot at the world's biggest and most efficient building. Make it better.” The default design is the shared challenge entry. The loop is open → propose a scoped change → simulate → AI evaluates the evidence → accepted change becomes the current city. A public claim of an actual world record or global efficiency ranking needs a defined metric and evidence; the design ambition may be stated boldly without fabricating that proof.

Participants can improve architecture as well as equipment. The owner wants winning contributions to earn the city's real estate; the current interpretation is **virtual territory/control or attribution within the shared model**, not a transfer of actual property. Exact territory and collaboration rules remain a design decision before implementing authority/storage changes.

The AI judges whether a proposal improves the active design using actual EnergyPlus outputs and published constraints. EnergyPlus supplies physics results; it does not itself adjudicate land ownership, architectural taste or unsupported structural claims. Internal frozen before/after models and run receipts remain necessary evidence, but are not a collection of disconnected user-facing city forks. A stale proposal must be reevaluated against the current city before adoption.

Results disclose every changed assumption. Entries are evaluated on the agreed service/comfort boundary; removing residents, crop production, computing or event capacity is not quietly credited as an efficiency improvement. A proposal must account for effects on neighboring buildings, shared plants, shading and grid peaks rather than improving one parcel by exporting its penalty. Tradeoffs and the meaning of “better” must be visible in the verdict. A separate personal project can deliberately change the program without claiming a like-for-like challenge win.

The playing field needs a genuine geometric seed and reproducible reference, not a perfect design. First establish a small validated section of the chosen massing with real floor/space boundaries and inherited assignments; then scale the same operations. The accepted single-zone checkpoint remains untouched and is not mislabeled as a completed microcity model.

The design should be developed around an understandable physical organization: public ground/civic realm; event, service and technical districts; mixed inhabited height bands; gardens/solar terraces; shared skyplace and skyport. Exact placement and capacity are not frozen yet. The owner may deliberately change this organization during design.

### Working spatial concept

A public pedestrian fabric is the organizing layer, with several urban blocks rather than a single sealed mall. Street-facing shops, galleries, food and everyday services line it. Larger event/civic rooms and gardens draw people through it. Residential neighborhoods include SRO, apartment and larger-home choices, with affordability treated as a separate program/economic condition rather than a thermal material property.

Three stepped/tapered landmark buildings can rise from this fabric and connect to a shared skyplace. They need not contain every use; lower and midrise buildings make many functions easier to access and service. South-facing photovoltaic terraces/canopies and planted roofs express the energy design. Intermediate sky streets/gardens keep public life from existing only at the top.

Below ground, test a service/vehicle network, parking and electric charging, then separated research/refuge spaces where geology, access and life safety permit. The optional rail concourse connects public circulation; plant, freight and waste routes do not cross audience/residential circulation indiscriminately. The skyport is a separate arrival/service node on the shared upper landscape, not an unbounded intrusion into its gardens.

Video windows provide an additional view experience for interior spaces. Real daylight courts, physical outdoor access and ventilation are evaluated separately. Public-facing screens can show actual selected simulation results, but no display or city animation is evidence that an energy calculation has executed.

These are program candidates, not twenty-seven newly implemented OSM templates. Some legacy broad categories can seed several detailed subtypes; others need sourced schedules/process definitions. Prices and marketing labels do not create new physics.

| Grocery | Sales, refrigeration, food preparation and recovered condenser heat |

| Commercial kitchens and food production | Process equipment, fuel, capture/exhaust and operating schedules |
| Convention and exhibition | Event diversity, setup/teardown, crowd densities and temporary equipment |
| Concert and immersive event venue | Show/AV loads, audience comfort, backstage, acoustics outside E+ and independently usable concourses |

## Reference cases

1. **Realistic normal:** a documented conventional design for the same location, program, service levels and floor-area accounting. It must be credible, not deliberately poor to make technology look good.
2. **90.1 reference:** choose an edition and comparison method explicitly. Standard90.1 is an energy-efficiency baseline, not a maximum-performance ceiling. Installed90.1 library selections are not a complete AppendixG baseline. Nonrotated research comparisons and formal PRM runs must remain separate; the owner's nonrotated research preference is preserved.
3. **Entrant designs:** one declared change/package at a time, with individual and combined effects retained. Participants can challenge assumptions or supply better data; accepted corrections create a new benchmark version, not retroactive edits to old results.

The UI selects which completed reference is the baseline and compares one entrant. Each climate track has its own reference; relocating from Vegas to LA is not counted as an efficiency saving.

## Fair comparison boundaries

- Fix weather/design conditions, occupancy/service schedules, floor-area accounting, comfort/IAQ requirements, utility boundary and reference version before scoring a round.
- Separate a fixed-geometry technology round from a morphology round. A morphology entry must retain the agreed service/program and area accounting; a smaller building cannot quietly win by doing less.
- Separate gross building energy from net grid energy after PV/export. Include pumps, fans, IT, crop lighting, dehumidification, displays, event AV, vertical transport and skyport charging where inside the boundary.
- Show annual energy, peak demand, time-of-use/demand cost, comfort/unmet hours, water by source/consumption, useful heat recovery and renewable production separately. No arbitrary all-in-one winner score until weighting is agreed.
- Food-production and computing functions need service denominators: crop type/yield/quality and IT service/load, not just area. Model equal services or disclose the difference.
- Record embodied/replacement impacts and installed-cost assumptions when supporting data exist; do not fabricate them to complete a dashboard.
- Maintain exact engine/model/input/weather/run identities. Original SQL remains read-only. Compare the same keys, units, frequency, environment and aligned time; absent channels stay absent.

## Candidate package and research status

- PCM: Armstrong Templok assemblies and the relevant Insolcorp lineage; the owner's legacy PCM work; an independently documented organic reference such as RubithermRT24HC. No named product enters as an invented generic material curve.
- Solar: south-facing roof/terrace/canopy PV with actual geometry, shade, temperature and inverter/loss inputs. Annual-yield and late-day-demand-optimal orientations may differ.
- Envelope: external shading, selective glazing and daylight-responsive lighting, with real daylight/glare constraints.
- Thermal systems: occupancy/pressure-zoned heat-recovery plant, heat pumps and thermal storage; all auxiliary electricity/water included.
- Planting: biosolar terraces as energy/water/landscape systems, not a presumed large PV-efficiency multiplier.
- Data-center heat: useful only when temperature, timing and receiving demand support recovery; the LED farm may itself need cooling.
- Radiative sky cooling: a separately quantified candidate with a sky-view/area budget, not a promise of cooling a megatall from its small roof.
- Advanced/research technology has a separate evidence label from established commercial equipment. An unvalidated model is not upgraded by an attractive rendering.

IESVE is a potential independent entrant/reference engine. Its documented Armstrong-specific PCM implementation is not automatically equivalent to EnergyPlus hysteresis. Cross-engine differences should be investigated, not averaged away or treated as proof one engine is ground truth.

## Public challenge and contributions

Invite modelers, manufacturers, researchers and designers to improve the field and submit reproducible changes. Provide a starting model, assumptions, weather references/permissions, output requests, run instructions and comparison definition before claiming the challenge is ready to download.

A contribution identifies the current shared-city revision, target territory, changed inputs/code/data, intended mechanism, supporting sources, execution environment, native diagnostics and results. Accepted improvements become the current shared entry; frozen evidence remains inspectable. Proprietary curves may be used only with appropriate permission; publish the rights status. Do not imply manufacturer endorsement or affiliation.

The public framing is a design/modeling challenge, not a claim that AI built the world's tallest structure or achieved an unrun savings percentage. The owner's LinkedIn discussion with BobFassbender motivates the invitation; no external post or contact is authorized by this draft.
